Summary

Question: A Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) trained on a person’s images (persona-LoRA) controls the persona’s identity fidelity in images generated by a base model like FLUX.1-dev, while a ControlNet guided by a control image controls their spatial structure. Applied together, do the two conflict, what does each trade, and which conditioning strengths are optimal?

Design:

  • Conditioning: FLUX.1-dev with a persona-LoRA and a ControlNet conditioned on the template. Five configurations vary, the guidance scale (gs), the LoRA scale (ls) and the canny, depth and pose scales (cs, ds, ps), plus the seed; the three ControlNet scales are the control modes and their total the conditioning sum.

  • Scoring: Identity fidelity is scored by a face-similarity service against held-out reference photographs, 0–100 (face_sim); template adherence compares the image with the template on edges (canny_f1), depth (depth_corr) and pose (pose_sim).

  • Search: Two branches, one per ControlNet implementation, the Union-Pro ControlNet and the FLUX.1 Control LoRA (control-LoRA), in stages, each a set of configuration grids (sweeps) scored and refined before the next; twenty-one sweeps, 2,716 images, nine personas and 21 templates in all. Every score is compared with measured floors, ceilings and seed noise.

Findings: All but the last group are about the Union-Pro ControlNet.

Guidance and LoRA scales:

  • Guidance changes both axes in the same direction, frozen at 6: Identity increases and template adherence is preserved or increases until 6, and most of the step-to-7 gains do not clear the seed noise (Section 6.1).

  • No strong evidence for a LoRA scale other than the trained 1.0, frozen there: Increasing it decreases identity and edge fidelity together, and every change inside the refinements is inside its seed noise (Section 6.1).

Conditioning mode: canny alone:

  • The scale induces the trade-off; the optimal grid is cs ≈ 0.40: Increasing it decreases identity and increases template adherence in every stage; at 0.40 identity is still usable and the structure scores stop increasing beyond seed noise (Section 6.1, Section 6.9).

  • A higher scale increases seed noise on identity and suppresses it on depth and pose: At cs 0.60 only four of ten seeds retain the usable 80 face_sim (Section 6.2).

  • One value is not suitable for every persona-LoRA: The same step increases structure for all six but decreases identity unevenly, from none to a large loss, so a set of values selected on the measured scores carries forward (Section 6.3).

Conditioning mode: canny with depth:

  • The optimal grid is the frozen quadrant, which persists across personas and templates and cannot be restricted to one cell: With both scales at 0.25 or 0.30 (frozen quadrant), the per-sum mean identity stays usable at every conditioning sum and every persona-LoRA and template retains the trade-off’s direction; further restriction to one cell alone fails the structure floors on about half of fifteen templates (Section 6.5).

  • The frozen quadrant ends one step before an identity cliff, at or below the floor of the model card’s recommended range: Inside the quadrant identity decreases moderately and past it collapses (identity cliff), by an amount that varies across persona-LoRAs and cannot be predicted from their identity before it. The quadrant with gs 6 and ls 1.0 (frozen configuration) retains identity for four of six persona-LoRAs; two decrease inside it (Section 6.9).

  • The template sets the absolute depth and pose scores; on any mode depth_corr is the weakest discriminator: In the frozen quadrant templates are further apart on pose_sim than the conditioning sum changes it; depth_corr’s small floor-to-ceiling span makes it a tie breaker only (Section 6.10).

Conditioning mode: canny, depth and pose:

  • The optimal grid is the conditioning sum band 0.80–0.85: The pose scale is at 0.5–0.6 and the other two at most 0.25 each; identity stays usable on all six templates while three are under a structure floor (Section 6.6).

The control-LoRA against Union-Pro:

  • Guidance changes neither axis; on Union-Pro it increased both (Section 6.8).
  • The canny trade-off recurs, past a threshold and with diminishing return, and the mean identity does not collapse at high canny scale, though single images do (Section 6.8).
  • At the same identity it retains more structure, with the steeper trade-off lines on the structure scores, but persona, template set and implementation change together between the branches, so the difference is not attributed to the implementation (Section 6.8.2).

Limits:

  • Single-seed findings hold as direction, not size: Four depend on one seed or on differences inside the seed noise: the guidance and LoRA-scale freezes, the fifteen-template cell check and the identity cliff, whose per-persona cells are single-seed. The rest is replicated across seeds and clear of its seed noise (Section 7).
  • Cross-persona identity gaps are partly the face model’s: The reference sets differ in source and quality across personas, so per-persona claims depend on within-persona contrasts, not on statements about the people photographed.
  • Cross-branch comparisons are shape comparisons only: The second branch conditions through a control-LoRA rather than a ControlNet, so its canny scale values do not compare with Union-Pro’s.

1 Research question

FLUX.1-dev is a text-to-image model: it generates an image from a prompt. A prompt can describe a person’s age, ethnicity, gender and other characteristics, but cannot reproduce the identity of a particular person; it can name a pose, standing, sitting or walking, but fine-grained control over spatial structure is beyond it.

A persona-LoRA trained on a specific person’s images solves the first problem: applied to the base model, it makes the generated person’s identity match the person trained on. It is also known as a subject, identity or character LoRA. Identity fidelity is whether the image retains the persona’s likeness.

A ControlNet solves the second problem: an auxiliary network trained on structural properties (conditioning controls) extracted from images, canny edges, depth maps or pose skeletons. At generation the controls from a control image accompany the prompt and the ControlNet injects its representations into the base model, so spatial structure follows the control image and appearance follows the prompt. Template adherence is whether the generated image retains the ad template’s edge, depth and pose layout.

The product goal: replace the person in an existing creative ad template with a persona-LoRA’s persona and retain the rest, pose, composition and scene. LoRA [1] adds a learned identity to a frozen diffusion model; ControlNet [2], its multi-condition successors [3], [4] and the FLUX.1 control-LoRAs [5] add spatial conditioning. None reports the two applied together, identity against structure across seeds, which this study measures.

The research question: applied together, do the two conflict, what does each conditioning trade, and which conditioning-strength values, or sets of values, are optimal? The study answers it in seven stages containing twenty-one parameter-grid sweeps, each scored on both axes, each stage freezing configurations for the next. Optimal here means the knee of the trade-off that satisfies both stopping rules (Section 5.10); every such set is a position on the trade-off, not a point free of it.

All prompts reinforce the template, none conflicts with it, so prompt adherence is not a third axis and the trade-off is between identity and structure.

2 Recent work

Two ad-creative systems set the context. AdBooster [6] outpaints a background around a fixed product image with Stable Diffusion, retaining the product pixels and selecting the scene from the user’s shopping interests; CG4CTR [7] generates ad creatives with Stable Diffusion inpainting in online experiments on a commercial ad platform and ranks them by predicted click-through rate from a reward model trained on observed clicks and impressions.

Identity also has zero-shot methods that take a reference photograph at generation instead of training weights: IP-Adapter [8] (decoupled cross-attention), PhotoMaker [9] (stacked identity embedding), InstantID [10] (face-embedding module plus an identity ControlNet) and PuLID [11] (contrastive alignment loss). On PuLID’s SDXL benchmark the identity-fidelity scores are 0.755 for InstantID, 0.722 for PuLID, 0.597 for IP-Adapter and 0.271 for PhotoMaker [11].

Against a persona-LoRA they compare on four axes. Identity: from one reference the strongest reach a fidelity InstantID reports as competitive with a LoRA [10]; a persona-LoRA learns from several and carries the face across angles. Compatibility with spatial control: each is an extra network with its own strength beside the ControlNet’s, whereas a persona-LoRA adds nothing at generation, so only the ControlNet’s strength is swept. Image quality: the injection-path methods show an AI-generated skin texture and oversaturation that a LoRA does not [12], and PuLID-FLUX trades image quality and prompt-following for fidelity [13]. Effort: a LoRA needs a training set and a training pass per persona, the zero-shot methods one image, or a few for PhotoMaker, and one forward pass. This study uses a persona-LoRA for compatibility and quality; its nine personas are trained once and reused across 2,716 images.

DreamBooth [14], whole-model subject fine-tuning, is the training objective behind most persona-LoRAs; the choice is one of form. On its benchmark the two forms reach the same subject fidelity, a DINO score of 0.614 against 0.613 on Stable Diffusion [15], with the LoRA training a small fraction of the parameters. The full-model form yields a base-model-sized checkpoint per persona, has no identity scale and cannot load beside a control-LoRA, so the LoRA form is used.

3 Methodology

3.1 Personas

The study uses nine persona-LoRAs, pf1–pf8 and pm1 (persona-female, persona-male). Personas pf1–pf8 are trained on well-known people, two of them on the same person, for research only: no generated image is published or used in an advertisement, the people are not identified, and no consent for commercial use of their identity is sought or implied; an advertisement would need it under right-of-publicity law.

3.2 ControlNets

Two ControlNet implementations are used. The Union-Pro ControlNet [4] is a single model carrying seven control modes, of which this study uses canny, depth and pose. The Black Forest Labs control-LoRA [5] is a LoRA rather than a separate network: it is loaded alongside the persona-LoRA in one pipeline, and it ships in canny and depth variants, of which only the canny one, FLUX.1-Canny-dev-lora, is used here.

3.3 Templates

The 21 templates are creative ad templates for fashion, product and lifestyle, each showing a male or female model posing with a product, coded tf1–tf15 and tm1–tm6 (template-female, template-male) by the subject’s gender. Each has a paired prompt describing its scene and product and naming the subject’s gender, so persona and template are matched: female personas with female templates, the male persona with male templates.

3.4 Reference data

Identity fidelity is assessed against a reference set: six reference photographs per persona, none used in training, every one with a clearly visible face across a range of events and lighting.

  • Candidates were collected from openly licensed and press sources, machine-clustered for identity, frontality-filtered, cropped to 1024×1024 at 2.2× the detected face box, and reviewed by manual inspection.

3.5 Prompt set

Each paired prompt is followed by a photorealism suffix. Some of the examples are:

  • female facing front, posing while holding credit card in the left hand, a photorealistic photo, 8k, hi res
  • female posing with coffee mug in the left hand, a photorealistic photo, 8k, hi res
  • male facing front, posing sideways while eating ice cream from a small cup with a spoon in the left hand, a photorealistic photo, 8k, hi res, plain background

A persona-LoRA needs its trigger word at generation only if its training used one. Five personas do (pf1, pf2, pf3, pf5 and pm1), and the generator supplies it by substituting the trigger word for the word female or male in the template prompt.

3.6 Scoring

3.6.1 Identity fidelity scoring

  • face_sim is scored by AWS Rekognition, a commercial face-similarity service.

  • Each image of a persona is scored against its six reference photographs in one call with the match threshold set to 0, so every reference photograph returns a similarity [16]. The reported face_sim value is their mean.

    \text{face\_sim}_m = \frac{1}{6} \sum_{j \in S_P} s(m, j) \tag{1}

    where s(m, j) is the similarity returned for image m and reference photograph j, and S_P is the reference set of the image’s persona P.

  • An image whose face the detector cannot find returns no match and is scored 0. 14 of the 2,716 scored rows had this issue.

3.6.2 Template adherence scoring

  • Edge fidelity: Measured by canny_f1, an F1 score. Edge maps are extracted from template and generation [17] and matched with a small dilation tolerance.
  • Depth-layout agreement: Measured by depth_corr. Monocular depth is estimated from both images by Depth Anything V2 [18] and compared by Pearson r, which is invariant to the scale and shift a monocular estimate is only defined up to.
  • Pose similarity: Measured by pose_sim. Body keypoints are detected in both images by YOLO11n-pose [19] and scored by an object keypoint similarity in the Common Objects in Context (COCO) form with COCO’s per-joint sigmas [20], at half the COCO tolerance and with the person’s box area as the scale.
  • An image in which the pose detector finds no person, or fewer than four joints in common with the template, has no pose_sim and is left out of every pose_sim mean; 5 of the 2,716 scored rows are such.

The three are cycle-consistency measurements: they re-extract from the output the controls the ControlNet consumed [2] and ask how much of the template’s structure persisted. This is the standard controllability measure of the ControlNet literature: ControlNet reports mean intersection over union (mIoU) between re-extracted and input segmentation maps, and Uni-ControlNet compares re-extracted edges, depth and pose to the input condition [2], [3].

4 Execution Plan

A sweep varies a few configurations, the rest held at values earlier stages froze, over a grid of cells, one per combination of the varied values; a cell’s score is the mean over every image generated at it. Each stage freezes one or more of its varied configurations for every later stage; every cell enters a reported mean.

The search ran as two branches: six stages and 19 sweeps on Union-Pro, one stage and 2 sweeps on the control-LoRA.

The columns:

  • Branch: One per ControlNet implementation, Union and control-LoRA. The scale values have different units on the two, so cross-ControlNet comparisons use their shapes instead of their values.
  • Stage: The Union branch’s stages are named U1–U6 and the control-LoRA branch’s C1.
  • Sweep Id: A sweep id is its stage code followed by an alphabetical count within the stage; the stages contain between one and eight sweeps each (Table 4).
  • Sweep variables: The configurations varied in the sweep, in column order; pf/pm is the persona, tf/tm the template and s the seed.
  • Images: How many images the sweep analysed.
  • Persona, Template, Seed, gs, ls, cs, ds, ps: The value(s) used: the persona(s), template(s) and seed(s), then the guidance, LoRA, canny, depth and pose scale value(s).

Table conventions:

  • Repeated values: Branch and Stage are written once per group, blank on the rows beneath.
  • Bold font: That configuration is varied in that sweep.
  • Green font: The value was frozen by an earlier stage; U5.b’s cell is one cell of the frozen quadrant. The seed is noise, not a configuration, so a single seed is never green.
  • Numeric value lists: Full when there are ≤ 3, otherwise the first two and the last, v1, v2, .. vn.
  • Em dash: That configuration was not active in that sweep.

5 Design

Separability: A staged search is valid only if the configurations frozen early do not interact strongly with those swept later. Nothing guarantees that, so the ledger at the end states, for each configuration, the separability assumption it relies on and the sweep that checks it; configurations suspected of interacting were swept together, hence canny and depth share one two-dimensional grid (screen). The values each configuration takes are in Table 4.

5.1 Persona

Which trained identity the persona-LoRA carries.

Values: One persona-LoRA, pf1, carries the search while the scales are located; six take over once there is a shape to validate across persona-LoRAs; and pm1 alone carries the three-mode stage and C1.a.

5.2 Template

The creative ad template the ControlNet conditions on.

Values: One template, tf4, is used through U4 so the scale sweeps are comparable; U5 opens to six and then the full female set, and U6 introduces the male set.

5.3 Seed

Not a configuration but the noise the configurations are measured against.

Values: Seven sweeps vary the seed, at three resolutions: widely spaced in U2.a, consecutive in U2.b, a few elsewhere; the other fourteen generate one seed per cell. Of the single-seed sweeps, the two that decide cells are repeated on more seeds: U4’s quadrant in U5.a and U6.g’s band cells in U6.h.

  • Multi-seed sweeps: U2.a, U2.b, U3, U5.a, U6.h, C1.a and C1.b
  • Single-seed sweeps: U1.a to U1.e, U4, U5.b and U6.a to U6.g

5.4 Guidance scale (gs)

FLUX.1-dev is guidance-distilled: the guidance scale is an input the model was trained to take in place of the classifier-free guidance weight, and no unconditional pass runs at inference [22]. Higher values follow the prompt more strongly, as they would under classifier-free guidance.

Values: U1’s two coarse grids interleave rather than repeat, from near-unguided to strong guidance across the model’s shipped default [22], and its refinements tighten on the top of that range; C1.a, on the control-LoRA, runs past every Union-Pro value, as the control-LoRAs’ providers recommend higher guidance.

5.5 LoRA scale (ls)

The LoRA scale controls the persona-LoRA’s contribution at generation time.

Values: The trained scale is 1.0; the coarse grids bracket it and the refinements tighten around it.

5.6 Canny scale (cs)

The canny scale is the primary ControlNet scale and the search’s main axis.

Values: Varied in nearly every sweep, it is walked down from the coarse grids to the quadrant with the step shrinking twentyfold: coarse-to-fine spacing places cells where the response changes, not where it is flat [23]. U2.b retains the wide grid while seeds vary, U4 re-centres it downward once depth carries part of the conditioning, and U6 walks it down again with pose added.

5.7 Depth scale (ds)

The depth scale sets the strength of the second control mode.

Values: No sweep before U4 conditions on depth. U4’s grid brackets the hypothesis that two control modes each need roughly half the single-mode scale: canny alone reaches its high structure scores near cs 0.6 in U2.b, so the joint grid centres both scales near 0.3; U5 retains that quadrant, and U6 sweeps depth again alongside canny and pose.

5.8 Pose scale (ps)

The pose scale sets how strongly the template’s pose skeleton conditions generation; it is the one configuration only the last Union stage, U6, conditions on.

Values: Swept inside U6 only, jointly with cs and ds.

5.9 Summary ledger

5.10 Stopping rules and evidence tiers

Two rules bound the search:

  • Structure floors: A cell whose canny_f1, depth_corr or pose_sim mean is under the floor (Section 11.3.2) has not retained the template, whatever its identity.
  • The usable line: A cell whose face_sim mean is under the usable 80 has not retained the persona, whatever its structure.

A configuration is retained where both rules pass, and the trade-off is measured only inside that region.

Every sweep carries an evidence tier under its plan row:

  • Single-seed: One seed per cell, every change measured against the seed noise borrowed from the nearest multi-seed sweep.
  • Replicated: Several seeds per cell.

Neither tier is confirmatory in the sense of a hypothesis fixed before the data: an adaptive search is exploratory throughout [24], [25]. The Summary’s Limits name the single-seed findings.

6.1 The U1 stage: Guidance, LoRA and canny scales on one persona

Question: With one persona on one template: which of guidance scale, LoRA scale and canny scale changes identity fidelity and template adherence, in which direction, and where is the usable region?

6.1.1.1 Guidance scale

  • Identity and template adherence increase together, higher is better: Increasing gs 1 → 3 → 5 triples face_sim and increases canny_f1 by half, while depth_corr and pose_sim change, so there is no trade-off and the higher gs value is selected.

6.1.1.2 LoRA scale

  • Mixed results, neither direction from 1.0 is preferred:
    • Increasing ls 0.8 → 1.0 → 1.2 decreases face_sim a few points and canny_f1 a few hundredths while depth_corr and pose_sim increase, so the refinements converge towards 1.0.

6.1.1.3 Canny scale

  • Identity decreases as each of the structure scores increases significantly: the trade-off:
    • Increasing cs 0.3 → 0.5 collapses face_sim while each of the structure scores changes: identity lost well under the usable 80, so the refinement stays inside this range.
    • Increasing cs 0.5 → 0.7 decreases face_sim further and increases each of the structure scores: template adherence increases only here.

6.1.1.4 The structure scores against face_sim, as the canny scale increases

  • The coarse step loses identity before it gains structure, so the usable region is under cs 0.5:
    • Changing cs 0.3 → 0.5, face_sim decreases 81.8 → 21.6 while canny_f1 and depth_corr change by a few hundredths and pose_sim increases 0.26 → 0.42.
    • Changing cs 0.5 → 0.7, each of the structure scores increases, canny_f1 the most, 0.25 → 0.52, with identity already lost.
    • The step is too wide to locate the region, which U1.c refines around cs 0.4.

6.1.2 U1 decision

  • Guidance scale — both axes change in the same direction; frozen at 6: In every sweep increasing gs increased face_sim and increased or preserved canny_f1, pose_sim never decreasing beyond a few hundredths, so the higher value is taken wherever its gain clears the seed noise. 7 was the top of the range tried between U1.a and U1.e, but most of its gains did not clear the seed noise, so 6 was frozen as its gain did clear.
  • LoRA scale — every change inside its seed noise but one; frozen at 1.0: In the coarse grids, ls < 1.0 gives slightly higher face_sim and canny_f1 and ls > 1.0 slightly lower, a decrease in both as ls increases that is inside the seed noise at every step, only U1.b’s whole-range canny_f1 decrease clear of the seed noise, while the refinements around 1.0 change nothing, so there is no clear evidence for any value other than 1.0.
  • Canny scale — identity decreases as the structure increases; cs ≈ 0.4 is the optimal grid for canny alone and is carried forward: In every sweep increasing cs decreased face_sim while increasing canny_f1, in the coarse grids each of the structure scores increased, which indicates a trade-off. Past cs 0.40 identity decreases in every sweep, and across the last refinements, 0.39–0.47, the structure scores no longer increase clear of the seed noise; no positive return interval excludes zero, so 0.40 is carried forward on that pattern.

U2 next measures how much of U1’s canny evidence is seed noise before cs ≈ 0.40 is carried forward.

6.2.2.1 Canny scale

  • The gains in the structure scores persist under seed stress; the identity value does not: Across cs 0.40 → 0.60 each of the structure scores’ change is well clear of its seed noise; face_sim’s decrease is marginally clear.
  • Identity’s seed noise grows as depth’s and pose’s shrinks; canny’s does neither: Negligible at cs 0.40 and 0.45, face_sim’s seed noise then widens to tens of points, while canny_f1’s ends about where it started and depth_corr’s and pose_sim’s shrink to about a third of their width; every seed is usable at 0.40 and 0.45, four of ten at 0.60.
  • cs 0.40 and 0.45 persist under seed stress, from 0.50 upward no value does: From cs 0.50 upward face_sim’s seed noise is wider than the step between neighbouring canny values, so a single-seed sweep, judged against this borrowed noise, cannot resolve neighbouring values there.

6.2.3 U2 decision

7 Threats to Validity

7.1 Single-seed grid cells

Generation is stochastic per seed, so a cell-level comparison needs several seeds averaged, not one sample each [26]. Four findings were not re-run on several seeds, and they are the threat:

  • The guidance and LoRA-scale freezes: Both depend on the same end-to-end direction across many independent series, never on a cell.
  • The position of the cliff: No multi-seed sweep reaches past the quadrant, so where identity collapses depends on U4’s single-seed cells; the quadrant itself, one step inside, is the multi-seed part.
  • The fifteen-template cell check: That the quadrant cannot be restricted to (cs 0.25, ds 0.30) depends on U5.b, one seed on each of its 105 images (Section 6.5).

The borrowing of sd_seed itself depends on two assumptions the study does not test: that a single-seed sweep’s seed is exchangeable with the seeds of the sweep that lends sd_seed, and that the seed noise at the lender’s nearest measured value transfers to the borrower’s own value.

7.2 Selection bias: the scores that select the frozen configuration also report it

The cell that comes out top of a noisy search is partly the one whose noise decreased on the high side [27], so a selected configuration’s value is trustworthy only when it is scored again on images that took no part in the choice. The state of each decision:

  • The frozen quadrant is re-scored: Selected on U4’s single-seed cells and re-scored by U5.a on fresh seeds and templates. On the fresh seeds the quadrant’s order by conditioning sum persists and the selection luck at the top is small, so the top cell’s U4 value is an upper bound and the two sum-0.55 cells are not separable.
  • The U6 band is re-scored on seeds: It was selected on U6.g’s single-seed cells, and U6.h retains those cells and adds two fresh seeds. On the fresh seeds alone the band’s face_sim mean is 97.3, against 98.4 on the seed it was selected on.
  • The guidance and LoRA-scale freezes are not re-scored: The scores quoted for gs 6 and ls 1.0 are the single-seed U1 grids’ own, so the bias there is unmeasured and those values are upper bounds.

7.3 The usable line

The usable face_sim of 80 is the scorer’s default match threshold, not a value the study derived [28], so every decision that names it was recomputed with the usable line at 70, 75, 85 and 90. Every decision remains valid for a usable line ≤ 80; for a usable line > 80 the frozen quadrant starts losing cells, because its own face_sim means exceed the line by a few points.

7.4 Images with no detectable face

Fourteen of the 2,716 rows have no detectable face, score 0 on face_sim and stay in every mean. Removing them instead changes no decision.

7.5 Multiple comparisons

Across the sweeps the report quotes returns and treats an interval that excludes zero as evidence of a trade; 20 of the 27 intervals do (one of them negative), and 19 still do after Holm’s correction [29]; the one the correction removes is the negative one. No decision depends on one interval, nor on the single-seed change claims, which are judged one at a time against two sds of a difference without correction, so about one in twenty of those marked clear could be noise.

7.6 Face-model demographic differentials

Every face_sim value inherits the face model’s behaviour across demographics, and the documented differentials are large, up to two orders of magnitude in false-match rates across groups [30], [31]. The three lowest-scoring persona-LoRAs are the two East-Asian people, face_sim was not characterised per demographic here, and the reference sets differ in source, so cross-persona face_sim gaps may partly reflect the face model: per-persona claims lean on within-persona contrasts.

Whether face_sim separates personas on generated images was checked on the 457 images of U3, U4, U5.a, U5.b and C1.b that were also scored against a second persona’s reference set, as the share whose own set scores higher (Equation 3):

\text{share} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^{M} \mathbf{1}\left[\text{own}_m > \text{second}_m\right] \tag{3}

where \text{own}_m and \text{second}_m are the two face_sim values of image m, M = 457, and \mathbf{1}[\cdot] is 1 when the condition holds and 0 otherwise.

The own set scores higher on nearly every image, and on all but a few once the pf6-against-pf8 ties are set aside (the two share one reference set); arcface_sim gives the same share, and against all nine reference sets the own set scores highest on nearly every image it scores.

7.7 Sequential coverage

A staged search only generates where earlier stages pointed; regions scored out early were never revisited at later fidelity.

7.8 No persona-LoRA-free control

No sweep generates without a persona-LoRA, so the ControlNet’s structure transfer is never measured free of persona-LoRA interference; the U1 LoRA-scale series are the only probe of it, and they bound the direction, not the size.

Two more are of the same kind: no sweep generates with the persona-LoRA and no ControlNet, so the structure the template alone would have retained is unmeasured, and no other identity method than a trained LoRA was run, so the trade-off is measured for persona-LoRAs only.

7.9 Incommensurable stages

The two branches condition by different mechanisms, so their cs values are different quantities sharing a name and are never compared. What is compared is shape: the control-LoRA’s canny-only curves are steeper, and Section 6.8.2 does not attribute that to the implementation, because persona gender and template gender change together, so U6, C1.a and C1.b each differ from the lineage in at least two respects. The sets change the slope too: within U5.a the return varies fourfold across templates and sixfold across personas, so a cross-branch ratio of eight to ten is within what a change of sets produces.

8 Conclusion

  • The study gives three optimal grids, one per control-mode set: Canny alone at cs ≈ 0.40 (ls 1.0, gs 6); canny with depth at the frozen quadrant, cs and ds at 0.25–0.3; all three at the conditioning sum band 0.80–0.85, the pose scale at 0.5–0.6. Each was selected on one seed and re-scored on several, the quadrant on both axes (Section 6.1, Section 6.4, Section 6.6).
  • The identity cliff is the defining measurement: The structure scores alone would drive the scales higher in almost all the cases; face_sim collapses once the ControlNet scales sum to 0.65, and the frozen quadrant is one step inside that boundary, where the two axes trade best.
  • One conditioning strength cannot fit every persona-LoRA: Every persona-LoRA decreases at the cliff, two of six clear of their single-seed noise, the loss spanning an order of magnitude and unrelated to identity before it; the weakest, pf6, degrades inside the quadrant. Hence best-of-N selection: generate across a set of conditioning strengths and retain the image the scores select.
  • What transfers is the procedure, not the values: Cycle-consistency structure scoring against the template, face_sim against held-out reference photographs, floors, ceilings and seed noise before any claim, and a selection rule with two stops, the structure floors and the usable face_sim 80. The scale values belong to these pipelines and these persona-LoRAs, and stay there.

Run the search for your own ControlNet and your own persona-LoRAs. Do not start from the numbers in this report.

11 Appendix: score calibration, item by item

Each quantity of Section 3.7 with its purpose, the data it uses, how it is computed and where it is used.

11.1 Working ranges

  • Purpose: The span the other anchors are compared against.

  • Data used:

    • Sweeps: all twenty-one.
    • Images: all 2,716 generated images.
    • Scores: all four scores.
    • Scored against: face_sim – own reference set, structure scores – own template.

    face_sim is 0 for the 14 images whose face the detector cannot find, and pose_sim is empty for the 5 images with no pose.

  • Computation method: Each score is ranked over all 2,716 study images, the empty pose_sim values left out, and the 5th and 95th percentiles are computed by linear interpolation between neighbouring ranks:

    h = (N - 1)\, p

    where N is the number of scores and p is 0.05 or 0.95; then, with the scores sorted as x_{(1)} \le \dots \le x_{(N)} and \lfloor h \rfloor the integer part of h,

    x_p = x_{(\lfloor h \rfloor + 1)} + \left(h - \lfloor h \rfloor\right)\left(x_{(\lfloor h \rfloor + 2)} - x_{(\lfloor h \rfloor + 1)}\right) \tag{4}

  • Place of use: The blue band of Figure 1.

11.2 Seed noise

11.2.1 sd_seed: estimated multi-seed noise of a score at one conditioning value

  • Purpose: The benchmark every later difference is measured against.

  • Data used:

    • Sweeps: all multi-seed sweeps.
    • Images: every image of these sweeps.
    • Configurations: persona, template, seed, gs, ls, cs, ds and ps.
    • Scores: all four scores.
    • Scored against: face_sim – own reference set, structure scores – own template.

  • Computation method: The images are grouped by persona, template and the value of every scale into the sets of images that differ only by seed, the seed groups. Inside each seed group, the sd of a score across its seeds:

    \bar{x}_g = \frac{1}{n_g} \sum_{i=1}^{n_g} x_{g,i}

    s_g = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n_g - 1} \sum_{i=1}^{n_g} \left(x_{g,i} - \bar{x}_g\right)^2}

    where x_{g,i} is the score of the image seed group g produced at its i-th seed, n_g is the number of seeds in the group and \bar{x}_g their mean. Then, at each conditioning value, the root-mean-square of that sd over the seed groups that share the value, the pooled sd:

    \text{sd}_{\text{seed}}(c) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{G_c} \sum_{g \in \mathcal{G}_c} s_g^2} \tag{5}

    where \mathcal{G}_c is the set of seed groups whose configuration has the conditioning value c and G_c their number (Figure 61): one number per sweep, score and conditioning value.

  • Place of use: The seed charts of U2.a and U2.b show two sd_seed either side of the mean; single-seed charts shade one sd_mean, and every single-seed change is compared against sd_mean.

11.2.2 sd_mean: the seed noise a single-seed sweep’s mean score at one conditioning value is measured against

  • Purpose: A single-seed sweep has no sd_seed of its own, so it borrows one to judge its differences against.

  • Data used:

    • Sweeps: all single-seed sweeps.
    • Images: every image of these sweeps.
    • Configurations: persona, template, seed, gs, ls, cs, ds and ps.
    • Scores: all four scores.
    • Scored against: face_sim – own reference set, structure scores – own template.
    • Key: the conditioning value c each per-value mean is computed at, cs, cs + ds or cs + ds + ps as per x-axis used in the trade-off charts.

  • Imported sd_seed: Each score’s sd_seed (Equation 5) at c^{*}, the lender’s measured value closest to the borrower’s c:

    • U1.a to U1.e: from U2, the pooled rows of U2.a and U2.b, which generated the same seed group at seeds that overlap at one, seed 6, so the cs 0.40 row pools 20 distinct seeds.
    • U4 and U5.b: from U5.a.
    • U6.a to U6.g: from U6.h.

  • Computation method: The imported sd_seed, divided by the square root of the number of images the single-seed mean averages, the sd of that mean:

    \text{sd}_{\text{mean}}(c) = \frac{\text{sd}_{\text{seed}}(c^{*})}{\sqrt{n}} \tag{6}

    where c is the borrower’s conditioning value, c^{*} the lender’s measured value closest to it and n the number of images in the mean at c.

  • Place of use: Shaded one sd_mean either side of each mean on every single-seed sweep’s charts, and the inside-the-noise rule (Equation 2) is applied wherever the report quotes a single-seed difference.

11.2.3 ci_mean: the 95% bootstrapped interval of a mean score at one conditioning value

  • Purpose: To show the variation of a score mean computed over several seeds.

  • Data used:

    • Sweeps: all twenty-one, drawn on the multi-seed sweeps’ charts.
    • Images: every image of these sweeps with the conditioning value c.
    • Configurations: persona, template, seed, gs, ls, cs, ds and ps.
    • Scores: one of the four scores.
    • Scored against: face_sim – own reference set, structure scores – own template.
    • Key: the value c of the factor the chart’s x axis varies, a conditioning value or gs, ls, persona, template or seed.

  • Computation method: A bootstrap 95% interval from resampling the seed groups with their images retained together [32]. Let G_c be the number of seed groups whose configuration has the conditioning value c. Take b = 1, \dots, B resamples of G_c of those groups with replacement, g^{*}_{b,1}, \dots, g^{*}_{b,G_c}, and compute the mean over their images:

    m_b(c) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{G_c} \sum_{i \in g^{*}_{b,k}} x_i}{\sum_{k=1}^{G_c} n_{g^{*}_{b,k}}}

    where x_i is the score of image i and n_{g} the number of images in group g. The interval is the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the B resampled means:

    \left[\, P_{2.5}(m_1(c), \dots, m_B(c)),\ P_{97.5}(m_1(c), \dots, m_B(c)) \,\right] \tag{7}

    with B = 2000 and P_q the q-th percentile, one interval per value. On a sweep with one seed per group, n_g = 1, this is the ordinary bootstrap over images.

  • Place of use: The per-value charts of multi-seed sweeps draw it as bars around each mean.

11.2.4 ci_return: the 95% interval of the return, the slope on a trade-off chart

  • Purpose: To say whether a trade between two conditioning values is resolved: a return whose interval spans zero is not.

  • Data used:

    • Sweeps: computed for U1.c, U2.b, U3, U4, U5.a, U6.a to U6.h, C1.a and C1.b; quoted where the face_sim change is resolved, in U1.c, U4, U5.a, C1.a and C1.b.
    • Images: every image of these sweeps.
    • Configurations: persona, template, seed, gs, ls, cs, ds and ps.
    • Scores: all four scores.
    • Scored against: face_sim – own reference set, structure scores – own template.
    • Key: the conditioning values c_0 and c_1 the return runs between, cs, cs + ds or cs + ds + ps as per x-axis used in the trade-off charts.

  • Computation method: A return is the slope of a structure score against face_sim on a trade-off chart, between two conditioning values c_0 and c_1, with its sign selected so that a structure score gained as face_sim is lost is positive:

    r = \frac{a}{b}, \quad a = -\left(\bar{y}(c_1) - \bar{y}(c_0)\right), \quad b = \bar{f}(c_1) - \bar{f}(c_0) \tag{8}

    where \bar{y}(c) is the mean structure score and \bar{f}(c) the mean face_sim at c. The images are grouped into blocks by persona, template and every scale the axis does not set; the blocks are resampled with their images at both values retained together, B = 4000 times [32], and a and b recomputed on each resample. A single-seed sweep has no seed groups, so each per-value mean is resampled from a normal distribution centred on it with sd its sd_mean, B = 4000 times. The resamples give variances s_a^2, s_b^2 and covariance s_{ab}, and the interval is Fieller’s [33] for a ratio of two means:

    g = \frac{z^2 s_b^2}{b^2}

    \frac{1}{1 - g} \left[\, r - \frac{g\, s_{ab}}{s_b^2} \pm \frac{z}{|b|} \sqrt{s_a^2 - 2 r\, s_{ab} + r^2 s_b^2 - g \left(s_a^2 - \frac{s_{ab}^2}{s_b^2}\right)} \,\right] \tag{9}

    with z = 1.96. When g \geq 1, that is when b is within z standard errors of zero, the interval is unbounded. A return is quoted only where the interval of its denominator b, the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of its resampled values, excludes zero.

  • Place of use: A return is quoted in the text only where its interval excludes zero.

11.3 Ceilings and floors

11.3.1 Identity

11.3.1.1 Floor

  • Purpose: The score an impostor reaches: the bottom of the measurable range, where a different person’s reference photograph can score.

  • Data used:

    • Images: the 54 reference photographs, six per persona, 48 of them distinct since pf6 and pf8 share one reference set.
    • Scores: face_sim.
    • Scored against: each of the eight other personas’ reference sets, pf6 and pf8 excluded from each other, since they share one reference set.

  • Computation method: The impostor comparison of biometric evaluation [28]. The impostor score of a reference photograph k against an impostor persona Q is the mean face_sim computed using Q’s reference set S_Q with reference photographs j \in S_Q:

    \text{imp}_{k,Q} = \frac{1}{6} \sum_{j \in S_Q} s(k, j) \tag{10}

    The floor is the 90th percentile of \text{imp}_{k,Q} over the 420 pairs.

  • Place of use: The usable 80 is > the floor; the floor lane of Figure 1 and the face_sim row of Table 2.

  • Result: Mean 2.9, 90th percentile 4.1, the floor, highest 52.2.

11.3.1.2 Ceiling

  • Purpose: The score a genuine reference photograph reaches: the top of the measurable range, which no generated image can be expected to exceed.

  • Data used:

    • Images: the 54 reference photographs, six per persona, 48 of them distinct since pf6 and pf8 share one reference set.
    • Scores: face_sim.
    • Scored against: own reference set with the match to itself removed.

  • Computation method: The genuine comparison of biometric evaluation [28]. For a persona P, its reference set S_P and a reference photograph k \in S_P, the genuine score of k is its mean face_sim against the other five:

    \text{gen}_k = \frac{1}{5} \sum_{j \in S_P,\ j \ne k} s(k, j) \tag{11}

    The ceiling is the mean of \text{gen}_k.

  • Place of use: The ceiling marker of Figure 1 and the face_sim row of Table 2; the usable 80 is 19 points short of it. With the floor (Equation 10) it bounds the no-error interval, above every impostor pair and below every genuine reference photograph, on which the usable-line check (Section 7) depends.

  • Result: Mean 99.3, the ceiling, lowest 87.9; the no-error interval is 52.2 to 87.9.

11.3.2 Structure

11.3.2.1 Floors

  • Purpose: The first stopping rule: a score under its floor is in the range where an unrelated image can get a similar score, so it has not adhered to the template (Section 5.10).

  • Data used:

    • Templates: the 21 templates.
    • Scores: the 3 structure scores.
    • Scored against: eight randomly sampled images generated using some other template.

  • Computation method: For a template T and one of eight randomly sampled generations for it u, the floor of a score is the 90th percentile over such 168 pairs:

    \text{floor} = P_{90}\left\{\text{score}(T, u)\right\} \tag{12}

    where P_{90} is the percentile by the same linear interpolation as the working ranges (Equation 4).

  • Place of use: The stopping rule; the U5.b charts draw the floor as a red line and the U6 sweeps test each score against it; Table 2 and Figure 1 carry the values.

  • Result: The floors are canny_f1 0.277, depth_corr 0.554 and pose_sim 0.156; depth_corr’s is the highest because unrelated generations already correlate, 0.34 on average, most ads sharing a centred-subject layout, which is why it is the weakest discriminator (Section 6.10.3).

11.3.2.2 Ceilings

  • Purpose: A validity check that structure scoring, the edge, depth and pose extraction and the comparison, adds no noise of its own: a template against itself must score 1.0. Not an achievable target: the achievable ceiling, what the pipeline reaches with the ControlNet and no persona-LoRA, is the control the study lacks (Section 7.8).

  • Data used:

    • Templates: the 21 templates.
    • Scores: the 3 structure scores.
    • Scored against: the template itself.

  • Computation method: The template against itself by the same scoring pass:

    \text{ceiling} = \text{score}(T, T) \tag{13}

    for each template T.

  • Place of use: The ceiling marker of Figure 1 and the pose_sim span in Section 6.10.

  • Result: A score of exactly 1.0 for all three on every template.

11.4 Resolvable difference

  • Purpose: To size the smallest effect a score can resolve at a given seed count.

  • Data used:

    • Sweeps: all multi-seed sweeps.
    • Configurations: the conditioning value, 29 in all.
    • sd_seed: for four scores at each value.

  • Computation method: The minimum detectable effect between two cell means at 80% power and a two-sided 5% alpha [34], [35]:

    \Delta_{\min} = \left(z_{0.975} + z_{0.80}\right)\ \tilde{\sigma}\ \sqrt{\frac{2}{n}} = 2.80\ \tilde{\sigma}\ \sqrt{\frac{2}{n}} \tag{14}

    where z_q is the q-th quantile of the standard normal distribution, n the number of seeds per cell, and \tilde{\sigma} the pooled sd_seed over the 29 conditioning values, the root mean square, the same pooling rule as sd_seed itself (Equation 5):

    \tilde{\sigma} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{29} \sum_{c} \text{sd}_{\text{seed}}(c)^2}

  • Place of use: Section 6.10, where the ten seeds a 0.2 pose_sim effect needs come from it.

11.5 Variance shares

  • Purpose: To rank which factor changes each score.

  • Data used:

    • Sweeps: all twenty-one, then U5.a and U3 on their own.
    • Images: all 2,716 generated images.
    • Configurations: branch, persona, template, seed, gs, ls, cs, ds and ps.
    • Scores: all four scores.
    • Scored against: face_sim – own reference set, structure scores – own template.

  • Computation method: The increase in R² a factor adds to a one-hot least-squares model of the other factors, the semi-partial R² [36]. For a least-squares fit of one score s on a set of factors, fitted separately for each of the four scores,

    R^2_s = 1 - \frac{\text{SSE}_s}{\text{SST}_s}

    where \text{SSE}_s is the sum of squared residuals of the fit and \text{SST}_s the sum of squared deviations of the score from its mean; the share of factor f in score s is

    \text{share}_{f,s} = R^2_{s,\ \text{all factors}} - R^2_{s,\ \text{all factors except } f} \tag{15}

  • Place of use: The U5.a finding (Section 6.5) and the variance subsection of Section 6.10.

11.6 Second identity scorer

  • Purpose: To show the identity axis does not depend on the one commercial scorer.

  • Data used:

    • Sweeps: all twenty-one.
    • Images:
      • Generated: the generated images of these sweeps, all 2,716.
      • Genuine and impostor: the 54 reference photographs, six per persona, 48 of them distinct since pf6 and pf8 share one reference set.
    • Scores: face_sim, and arcface_sim from an ArcFace embedding [37].
    • Scored against:
      • Generated: own reference set.
      • Genuine: own reference set with the match to itself removed.
      • Impostor: each of the eight other personas’ reference sets, as in the Floor.

  • Computation method: The embeddings come from InsightFace’s buffalo_l pack [38], run on CPU. The ArcFace similarity of two images a and b is the cosine similarity of their embeddings \mathbf{e}_a and \mathbf{e}_b:

    \text{arcface}(a, b) = \frac{\mathbf{e}_a \cdot \mathbf{e}_b}{\lVert \mathbf{e}_a \rVert\ \lVert \mathbf{e}_b \rVert} \tag{16}

    Only the scoring method changes: \text{arcface}(a, b) takes the place of s(a, b), and the rest is the same.

  • Place of use: Everywhere a face_sim score is used to make a claim.

  • Result:

    • Generated images, pairs of conditioning values ordered the same way by both scores, per stage: A pair is ordered the same way when both mean scores put the same value higher; over the twenty sweeps with more than one value, 288 of 315 pairs are (Table 84).

    • Generated images, Spearman rank correlation of face_sim and arcface_sim: Over the 2,690 images for which both scores are available, their Spearman rank correlation is 0.90.

    • Genuine and impostor, one score at a time: The statistics face_sim already has, computed the same way on arcface_sim, are in Table 85.
